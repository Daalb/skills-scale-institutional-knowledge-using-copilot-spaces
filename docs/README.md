# OctoAcme Project Management Docs

This documentation describes OctoAcme's approach to managing cross-functional projects from initial validation through delivery, release, and continuous improvement. The intent is to give teams a common operating model, shared artifacts, and a consistent way to communicate progress, risks, and decisions.

## Project management processes

OctoAcme uses a lightweight, iterative lifecycle that begins with initiation and ends with retrospectives and improvement actions. Teams first validate the business need, success metrics, stakeholders, and level of commitment required to proceed. After approval, they plan scope, milestones, resource needs, dependencies, risks, and a prioritized backlog with clear acceptance criteria. During execution, work is tracked on a project board, progress is reviewed in daily and weekly rhythm, and blockers are escalated through established ownership paths. When work is ready, releases follow pre-release checks, deployment verification, and rollback planning. After each sprint, release, or significant milestone, the team captures lessons learned and converts them into action items.

The process is designed around a few core principles: clear ownership, iterative delivery, customer value, measurable outcomes, and psychological safety. Project work is structured around a shared set of artifacts such as the project charter or one-pager, roadmap, backlog, risk register, acceptance criteria, and retrospective notes. This keeps planning, delivery, and reporting aligned across product, engineering, QA, and stakeholder groups.

## Key roles and responsibilities

OctoAcme relies on clearly defined personas to distribute accountability. The Project Manager coordinates planning, timelines, risk, communication, and cross-team execution. The Product Manager defines the problem, prioritizes backlog items, and measures business impact. Developers build and test features to meet acceptance criteria and quality standards. QA validates quality and release readiness. Stakeholders provide input, approval, and business context. These roles work together through project boards, backlog refinement, planning sessions, and delivery reviews.

## Communication and quality practices

Communication is regular and structured to keep teams and stakeholders aligned. OctoAcme emphasizes daily standups for blockers and progress updates, weekly delivery syncs for broader status and risk review, demos at milestone boundaries, and stakeholder updates at agreed cadences. Risk communication follows a clear escalation path from the team to the PM, Product Lead, and sponsor when needed. Quality is treated as a product requirement: unit tests, integration tests where useful, end-to-end smoke tests for critical paths, CI checks for tests and security scans, and manual QA where acceptance needs to be validated.
